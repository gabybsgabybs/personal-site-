AI case · Canva, 2025

Building evals to decide which AI video effects ship

A PM-owned eval system decided which effects shipped to Canva's international markets.

Flip for the case
ContextOur local team created locally relevant GenAI video effects, starting with a Catrina for Día de Muertos. With seasonal time pressure, I was responsible for making sure each effect met the quality bar our users expect, in time for the holiday. ActionBuilt a 7-dimension rubric with a binary Publish Ready call and rated 50–60 outputs per effect, hundreds in total, as sole approver. Shipped a known multi-face limitation because instructions set a single-face expectation and the campaign window mattered more. ResultEffects launched on schedule across markets, lifted publish rate 2pp and ranked among Canva's top AI video effects. LearningEven without perfect scores across the rubric, the effect launched and was a success. The tradeoff between accuracy and timing is real.

AI case · Canva, 2025

Designing GenAI for people who don't write prompts

A live GenAI feature that turns a theme and a colour into a finished social post for LatAm small businesses.

Flip for the case
ContextLatAm shop owners used to post the same promos and greetings on Instagram every week, built from templates. Now they don't want templates anymore: prompting became the new way to create content. ActionDesigned a UI on top of LLMs to create high-frequency, standardized content: pick a theme and a palette, get a finished post. Copy runs generate → LLM judge → human tone gate; images render live from a slot-based prompt with strict text rules and LoRA styles for variety. Held cost with per-user caps and plan-level AI credits. ResultA/B test lifted publish rate 5% and same-week return 2% across markets, at a cost per generation the business could sustain. LearningFor most people the best GenAI UX hides the prompt. The quality work lives in the pipeline (judge, tone gate, constrained prompts), not in asking users to get better at prompting.

Growth · PayPal, 2021

Tripling Pay Later through international expansion

Market-by-market regulatory adaptation and checkout friction removed.

Flip for the case
ContextPay Later was growing in a few core markets, while every new country brought its own credit regulation and checkout habits. The goal was five new markets and a larger first-time user base without hurting checkout conversion. ActionAdapted the product to each market's regulation, removed checkout friction through A/B tests, and ran discovery for the next growth bets and the long-term vision. ResultMonthly transactions tripled (2.7M to 8M), the business grew 50%, and checkout conversion rose 25 bps. LearningRegulation is a product input, not a legal checkbox. Designing the checkout around each market's rules, rather than retrofitting, is where the conversion gains came from.
